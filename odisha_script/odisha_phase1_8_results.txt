====================================================================================================
Null distributions (200 draws each)
====================================================================================================
  RANDOM-FEATURE : k-means(k=6) on N(0,1) stacks of 21 dims over the same 1249 units
  PERMUTATION    : shuffle of the observed unit->cluster labels (sizes preserved)
  SPATIAL        : k-means(k=6) on standardised unit centroids only -- STRICT

  The SPATIAL null exists because the first run of this script failed its own control.
  distance_to_water, never fed to k-means, cleared the random-feature p95 by +0.010.
  That is structural: the observed partition is spatially coherent and a random-feature
  partition is not, so within-cluster spread under the latter is near-global for every
  band and anything spatially smooth beats it. The random-feature null was asking 'is
  this band spatially autocorrelated', which all of them are. The spatial null gives the
  null partition the compactness the observed one has, so a band clears it only by
  separating clusters better than geography alone.

  CONTROL FIRST. distance_to_water was never fed to k-means -- clustering dropped it
  as a constant band -- so it is a null sample sitting inside the published chart.
    observed 0.089   random-feature p95 0.079   spatial p95 0.077

  It CLEARS the random-feature null. That null is therefore too permissive and
  no 'survives' verdict read off it is evidence.

  Under the SPATIAL null it does not clear comfortably either: excess +0.012,
  which is the second smallest in the table. It is a pure function of position, so a
  spatially-compact null should land almost exactly on it -- and does. That is the
  null being calibrated rather than passed.

  USE IT AS THE FLOOR. A known-null band scores +0.012 here, so any band whose
  excess is below about 0.012 is not distinguishable from it. On that reading the
  bottom of the SURVIVE list -- vv_p90 (+0.008) and vv_p50 (+0.014) -- should be read
  as null too, and the honest count is the bands clearly above that floor.
  Against the SPATIAL null: 17/22 bands separate the clusters better

What this says about the chart
====================================================================================================
  1. The caption is wrong. The unit is the k-means cluster, k=6, not the stand. It must
     read 'between-cluster', and the title must not say 'What separates the stands'.
  2. The ranking is not evidence on its own. Every band except distance_to_water was fit
     on, so a high ratio is the expected outcome of the construction.
  3. elevation's high rank is the clearest case. It is in the k-means feature vector, so
     its separation is partly circular -- and it is also the band most aligned with
     geography, which is what the spatial null is built to price in.
  4. Report the excess over the spatial null, not the raw ratio, if this chart is to
     support a claim about which features drive the partition.

  5. The band that dominates the partition is the band the field data trusts least.
     canopy_height and canopy_height_max lead the survivors at +0.268 and +0.264, well
     clear of everything else -- and Phase 1 measured ETH canopy height against 274
     Odisha plots at R2=0.207 with slope 0.30. canopy_height_std survives too (+0.076)
     while measuring crown cover at R2=0.000 (r=-0.011). So these bands genuinely drive
     the partition; what they do not do is measure the forest property they are named
     for. Those are not in tension -- a band can carve consistent groups out of a
     landscape while being a poor estimator of the quantity it claims -- but any claim
     that the clusters are structurally meaningful rests on the second, not the first.
