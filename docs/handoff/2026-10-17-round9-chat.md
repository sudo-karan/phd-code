### Task 2: the validation scores (code only)

Three new modules in `src/fmu/evaluation/`, each with tests on synthetic data worked by hand:

- `reference_height.py`. `stand_raster` puts stand polygons on a height raster's grid by the
  pixel-centre rule and refuses overlapping stands. `height_variance_explained` gives
  R2 = 1 - SS_within / SS_total of a reference height raster on stand membership, pixel-weighted:
  the explained variance of Xiong et al. (2024, section 2.7.2), computed through the existing
  `unsupervised.explained_variance`. It reports the share of height pixels with no stand beside R2.
- `stand_overlap.py`. `overlap_agreement` implements Vatandaslar et al. (2025, eq. 1-3): each
  reference stand is matched to the stand it overlaps most, with OLO = area(x∩y)/area(y),
  OLU = area(x∩y)/area(x), and overall = (OLO + OLU)/2, averaged over the pairs.
  `iou_agreement` implements Xiong et al. (2024): the IoU of each delineated stand with its
  reference, and the shares of stands with IoU > 0.5 and > 0.7.
- `torus_shift.py`. `torus_shift_test` is the torus translation of Harms et al. (2001) for a
  contiguous plot grid. Every whole-quadrat shift of the stand map over the fixed field grid is
  enumerated, with no seed. It returns the caller's statistic on every map, ForestGEO's
  greater/less/equal counts, and the package's (b + 1)/(n + 1) p. It is a test, not a metric. The
  existing `spatial_null.torus_translation_null` is a different tool: it draws shifts at random,
  for scattered plots.

**Task 2.** What the papers do not say. Each point is marked UNVERIFIED in the module and needs
your call, or Prof. Seth's, before these scores are declared for a site:

8. **Xiong's pairing.** Xiong et al. never say how a delineated stand is paired with "its
   corresponding reference". The module pairs it with the reference it overlaps most. A stand
   that overlaps no reference has IoU 0 and counts in the shares.
9. **Xiong's threshold.** The methods say "> 0.5 / > 0.7" and the results "≥". The module uses
   strictly greater, as the methods state it. The two differ only on exact ties.
10. **Ties for largest overlap** go to the candidate passed first. **Vatandaslar's unmatched
    references** (the paper reports none) are counted and left out of the means. **Vatandaslar's
    sample**: they assessed 62 randomly chosen reference stands; the module assesses every
    reference passed, and the caller samples.
11. *(waiting for the Harms 2001 PDF, Jaskaran 17 Oct)* **Harms 2001's rotations and
    reflections.** Did the 2001 paper also use rotated and mirrored copies of the habitat map?
    Harms's 2011 script does not. Until the PDF is read the default stays translations only, and
    the three other orientations of a rectangle are available when named.
12. **Stands on a quadrat grid.** `torus_shift_test` takes the stand of each quadrat as given. The
    rule for assigning a quadrat to a stand (by its centre, or by largest area) is not chosen
    here; it has to be declared with the site.
13. **The height R2.** A pixel counts if its centre lies in a stand, and both sums are over the
    pixels with a stand. Xiong takes the mean over the whole study area. The two are the same only
    where the stands cover the study area, and on kept land they do not. The share of height
    pixels with no stand is reported for that reason.
14. **`src/fmu/evaluation/__init__.py`** describes the package, but it is an existing file, so the
    three new modules are not listed in it this round. Two lines are needed when editing is
    allowed again.
