### What step 1 built

- **D2, the soft ceiling** (`merge.pass2_soft_ceiling`, `src/fmu/utils/region_merge.py`). With it
  on, a region still below `merge.min_area_ha` when its turn comes in the second pass takes, in
  Jaskaran's order (section 1b): a relaxed match that keeps the result at most `max_area_ha`, the
  closest; else a relaxed match over it; else the longest shared edge under `max_area_ha`; else
  the longest shared edge over it. Every result is capped at `max_area_ha + min_area_ha`, checked
  on each merge, so a stand already over the ceiling takes another region only within the cap.
  Pass 1 and a region that has reached the minimum keep the hard ceiling. `area_blocked` is filed
  against the ceiling that applied. `Config.max_component_pixels()` then derives the cap from
  `max + min` (480 px for the 3 ha boxes, 1,320 px for Sanjay Van's 10 ha). With the setting on,
  `merge_diagnostics` gains seven keys: the merges over `max_area_ha` by a relaxed match and by
  the fallback, how many stands end over it, how many of those are single superpixels that came
  in over it and never merged, and the excess (total, largest, and each stand's, largest first).
  Off, the merge is what it was: `tests/test_region_merge_soft_ceiling.py` compares it, on 516
  graphs, with digests written from the merge of `363ea6d`, and the 200 golden digests of
  `tests/test_region_merge_provenance.py` still hold.
- **D3, the specks out of the fit** (`clustering.fit_excludes_stands_below_min_area`,

  (`below_min` in `stand_provenance`, whatever its cause) are left out of the k-means training rows
  only. The skewness test, the log offsets and the robust scaling still take a row from every
  unit, because a stand left out of the sample whose minimum sets the log offset could get the log
  of a negative number, and then no type. They are left out with masks on the integer label image
  (remap), not by filtering the sampled rows by label: whether Earth Engine matches a list of
  integers against a sampled number is not established. `cluster()` still types every unit, so

   Why it matters. Superpixels are what the merge joins, and it treats each one as one region. A
   superpixel in two pieces can give a stand in two pieces. In all three cases one or two other
   superpixels touch both pieces along a side, so a stand that also takes one of them is in one
   piece: whether a stand ends in pieces depends on the merge, which has not run with the setting
   on.

   What the check did (`scripts/check_split_superpixels.py`, result
   `docs/reproducibility/snic_split_superpixels_kendujhar.json`): the segmentation A6 ran (the box
   run's cached upstream, the setting on in memory, small patches kept); the superpixels in more
   than one piece, from one grouped count over the ROI; then a window around each on the analysis
   grid (EPSG:32645, 10 m): the stage's label, SNIC's own label before the stage clips it to the
   ROI and masks it to kept land, kept land, the ROI, and A6's pieces.

   | Label | Pieces (as A6 counts them) | Area of each piece | What separates the pieces |
